## Concept

Air Force pilot John Boyd noticed that American pilots won more dogfights not because of better planes, but because they could cycle through decisions faster than opponents. He formalized this as OODA: Observe (gather data), Orient (make sense of it through your mental models and experience), Decide (choose a course of action), Act (execute). The pilot who loops fastest gets inside the opponent's decision cycle — by the time the enemy decides, you've already moved. In business and life, the competitor who iterates faster, learns faster, and adjusts faster wins — not necessarily the one with the best initial strategy.

## Key insight

Speed isn't just an advantage — it's a compounding one. Every loop produces information that makes the next loop faster and smarter. Slow loops don't just lose. They fall further behind with every cycle.

## Practice prompt

Help me design a faster OODA Loop system for a competitive or uncertain situation I'm navigating.

Situation: [DESCRIBE YOUR COMPETITIVE OR HIGH-UNCERTAINTY CONTEXT]

Walk me through all four stages with specific tactics:

OBSERVE — What data should I be monitoring? What signals matter? What am I currently ignoring that could be crucial? What's the most information-dense thing I could observe right now?

ORIENT — What mental models or frameworks should I use to make sense of this data? What biases might be distorting my current orientation? What does someone with a fundamentally different worldview see here?

DECIDE — Given my observations and orientation, what are my real decision options? What criteria should I use to pick quickly without overthinking? What's the minimum viable decision that lets me act and learn?

ACT — How do I execute quickly and create a feedback loop? How do I design my action so it generates maximum learning for the next loop?

Finally: Where in my current OODA loop am I slowest? What's creating the most friction? How do I cut that cycle time in half?

## A small challenge

Identify one area where you're slow to decide. What specific step (Observe/Orient/Decide/Act) is the bottleneck? Fix that one step this week.
