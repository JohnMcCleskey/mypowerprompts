## Concept

Feynman had a rule: if you couldn't explain something in simple terms, you hadn't actually understood it yet — you'd just memorized the vocabulary. His technique: take any concept you think you understand, write it out as if explaining to a child with zero background knowledge, then wherever you get stuck or resort to jargon, go back and actually learn that part. Do this until you can explain the entire concept without any specialized language. The gaps you find aren't embarrassing — they're exactly where the learning happens.

## Key insight

Jargon is how experts hide from each other and from themselves. Simplicity isn't a dumbing down — it's proof of mastery. The moment you can explain a complex thing simply is the moment you actually understand it.

## Practice prompt

Use the Feynman Technique to help me test and deepen my understanding of a concept, skill, or domain.

What I think I understand: [THE CONCEPT, IDEA, OR SKILL YOU WANT TO TEST]

Step 1 — MY EXPLANATION: Let me explain it first (I'll describe it below): [YOUR EXPLANATION IN SIMPLE TERMS]

Step 2 — GAP ANALYSIS: Based on my explanation, identify:
- Where did I use jargon or circular definitions instead of real understanding?
- What questions would a genuinely curious 12-year-old ask that I couldn't answer?
- What did I skip over or wave my hands at?
- What would break my explanation if tested?

Step 3 — THE GAPS: For each gap you identified, give me the actual clear, simple explanation of that thing — what I'm missing.

Step 4 — THE NEXT LEVEL: Once I understand those gaps, what deeper questions emerge that someone with real mastery would wrestle with?

Be a relentless curious child. Accept no jargon.

## A small challenge

Pick one concept from your work you explain regularly. Explain it out loud to an imaginary 12-year-old. Where do you reach for jargon? That's your next learning target.
