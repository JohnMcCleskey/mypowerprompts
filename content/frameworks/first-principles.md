## Concept

Most thinking is analogical — we copy what's worked before and make small tweaks. First principles thinking is the opposite: break a problem down to its most fundamental, undeniable truths, then reason back up from scratch. Musk famously applied this to rocket costs. Instead of "rockets are expensive because they've always been expensive," he asked: what are rockets actually made of? Steel, aluminum, copper. What do those materials cost? A fraction of the rocket price. So the existing pricing was pure accumulated assumption, not physical reality.

## Key insight

Most constraints you accept as fixed are actually inherited assumptions. When you find a constraint, ask: "Is this a law of physics, or is it a tradition?" Physics wins. Traditions can be redesigned.

## Practice prompt

Apply first principles thinking to the following challenge. Help me break it down to its most fundamental, undeniable truths — stripping away all assumptions, conventions, traditions, and "how it's always been done."

My challenge: [DESCRIBE YOUR PROBLEM OR GOAL]

For each assumption I currently hold, identify:
1. Is this a physical/logical necessity, or is it inherited convention?
2. What would a completely naive observer think if they saw this from scratch?
3. What's the actual base-layer cost/requirement once all assumptions are stripped?

Then build back up from first principles to propose three unconventional solutions that would be impossible to see while still inside the conventional framing.

Make me question things I've never thought to question.

## A small challenge

Pick one thing you believe is "just how it works" in your field. Write down 3 assumptions baked into that belief. Test each: is it physics, or is it tradition?
