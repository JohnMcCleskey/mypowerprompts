## Concept

A post-mortem happens after something fails and asks "what went wrong?" Klein's insight was to flip it: before a project launches, tell the team "It's one year from now and this project has failed completely. Why did it fail?" This technique — the pre-mortem — short-circuits groupthink and planning fallacy. In a regular planning meeting, people censor their doubts because the plan is already decided. In a pre-mortem, the failure is assumed, which gives people permission to voice their real concerns. It consistently surfaces risks that forward-planning misses.

## Key insight

Optimism is an asset when building. It's a liability when planning. The pre-mortem is a permission structure — it makes pessimism temporarily correct, so people share what they actually see.

## Practice prompt

I want to run a rigorous pre-mortem on a plan I'm about to execute.

My plan: [DESCRIBE YOUR PROJECT, DECISION, OR INITIATIVE]

Run the pre-mortem in four acts:

ACT 1 — THE FAILURE: It's [DATE 12 MONTHS FROM NOW] and this plan failed spectacularly. Not a minor setback — a clear, costly, embarrassing failure. Write the two-paragraph internal memo explaining what happened.

ACT 2 — THE KILLER RISKS: List the 7 most specific, realistic failure modes that could produce this outcome. Not vague risks like "execution issues" — specific ones like "we ran out of runway in month 4 because we underestimated customer acquisition costs by 3x."

ACT 3 — THE BLIND SPOTS: What risks are we not even considering because we're too close to the plan? What would an outside critic with full information say we're naively missing?

ACT 4 — THE INSURANCE: For each of the top 3 risks, what is the specific early warning signal I should monitor, and what's my contingency action if that signal fires?

Don't reassure me. Stress-test this plan until it either breaks or I'm confident it won't.

## A small challenge

Take your most important current project. Set a 10-minute timer and write every way it could fail. Don't censor. See what surfaces that you haven't acted on yet.
