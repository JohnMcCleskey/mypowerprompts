## Concept

Everyone has domains where they genuinely know what they're doing — and everywhere else, they're operating on borrowed maps. Buffett's entire investing philosophy is built on this: he only invests in businesses he truly understands, and refuses to chase opportunities outside that circle, no matter how attractive they look. The circle isn't fixed — you can expand it with deliberate study. But the most dangerous position is not knowing where the circle ends. Overconfidence in a domain you don't truly understand is how most people lose — in investing, in decisions, in life.

## Key insight

The size of your circle isn't what matters. Knowing the exact edge of it is everything. A small, well-defined circle beats a large, fuzzy one every time.

## Practice prompt

Help me rigorously map my circle of competence in a specific domain, and identify the edges where my knowledge becomes unreliable.

Domain I'm assessing: [YOUR FIELD, SKILL, OR DECISION AREA]

Please work through:

1. CORE KNOWLEDGE — What do I genuinely, deeply understand in this domain? What patterns have I seen repeatedly? What would I confidently bet on?

2. EDGE CASES — Where does my knowledge start to thin? What questions would expose gaps? What would I have to look up?

3. OUTSIDE THE CIRCLE — What looks adjacent but is actually beyond my reliable knowledge? Where am I most likely to be confidently wrong?

4. BLIND SPOTS — Based on how people typically build expertise in this domain, what common blind spots should I specifically watch for?

5. EXPANSION PATH — If I wanted to deliberately expand this circle in the next 90 days, what specific knowledge or experiences would move the edge?

Be ruthless. Flattery here is useless.

## A small challenge

Write down the 3 decisions you're most confident about right now. For each, write one thing that could be true that would completely change your confidence. Can you rule that thing out?
