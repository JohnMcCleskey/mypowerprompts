## Concept

When facing a decision clouded by emotion, Welch's rule cuts through by forcing you to evaluate across three time horizons simultaneously. How will you feel about this in 10 minutes? (Immediate emotional reaction — what your gut wants.) 10 months? (Medium-term consequences — career, relationships, momentum.) 10 years? (Long-term identity — who you'll have become.) The genius is that each horizon reveals something the others hide. The 10-minute frame exposes irrational urgency. The 10-month frame catches things the emotions obscure. The 10-year frame aligns decisions with who you actually want to be.

## Key insight

Most poor decisions are made in the wrong time frame. Emotional decisions optimize for 10 minutes while ignoring 10 years. Over-analytical decisions optimize for abstract futures while ignoring present reality. The 10/10/10 forces you to hold all three simultaneously.

## Practice prompt

Help me apply the 10/10/10 rule to a decision I'm struggling with, where my emotional state and long-term values feel like they're in conflict.

The decision I'm facing: [DESCRIBE THE DECISION AND THE OPTIONS YOU'RE WEIGHING]

What I'm feeling right now about it: [YOUR CURRENT EMOTIONAL STATE — fear, excitement, guilt, pressure, etc.]

Walk me through all three time horizons in depth:

IN 10 MINUTES (the emotional layer):
- What does my gut want to do right now?
- What emotion is driving that impulse — relief, excitement, avoidance, social pressure?
- Is this emotion giving me useful information, or is it noise?

IN 10 MONTHS (the consequence layer):
- Where will each option have taken me by then?
- What relationships, opportunities, or habits will be different?
- Which option am I more likely to be quietly relieved or quietly regretful about?

IN 10 YEARS (the identity layer):
- Who do I want to have become by then?
- Which decision aligns with that person's choices?
- Will the 10-minute emotional driver even exist in my memory in 10 years?

SYNTHESIS: What decision does all three horizons point toward? Where do they conflict, and how do I resolve that conflict?

## A small challenge

Next time you feel urgency about a decision, pause and ask: "Is this a 10-minute urgency or a 10-year one?" If it's 10-minute, wait one hour before deciding.
