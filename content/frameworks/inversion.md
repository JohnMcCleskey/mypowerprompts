## Concept

Instead of asking "how do I achieve X?", ask "what would guarantee I fail at X?" or "what would produce the opposite of what I want?" Then systematically avoid those things. Munger, Buffett's partner, said he learned more about how to live a good life by studying what makes lives go wrong than by studying success. The road to many great solutions is paved with understanding what not to do. Inversion bypasses optimism bias and reveals risks you'd never surface by thinking forward.

## Key insight

Success lists are inspiring but vague. Failure lists are specific and actionable. You can rarely guarantee you'll do everything right — but you can choose not to do the things that reliably guarantee failure.

## Practice prompt

I'm going to use inversion to stress-test my plan and find hidden failure modes.

My goal or project: [DESCRIBE WHAT YOU'RE TRYING TO ACHIEVE]

Step 1 — ASSUME FAILURE: Imagine it's one year from now and this has failed spectacularly. Write the obituary. What went wrong? Be specific and brutal.

Step 2 — CATALOG FAILURE MODES: List every action, habit, decision, or inaction that would reliably produce this failure.

Step 3 — INVERT: For each failure mode, what is the opposite action or protection?

Step 4 — PRECOMMITMENT: Which of these failures am I most likely to commit given my specific tendencies and weaknesses? Be honest.

Step 5 — DESIGN: Based purely on avoiding the failure modes, what does a bulletproof version of this plan look like?

Don't optimize for success. Optimize for not failing.

## A small challenge

Take your biggest current goal. List 5 things that would guarantee you fail at it. Now check — are you currently doing any of them?
