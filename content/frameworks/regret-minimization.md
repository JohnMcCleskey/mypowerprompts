## Concept

When Bezos was considering leaving a stable Wall Street job to start Amazon, he used a mental model he now calls the Regret Minimization Framework. He asked: when I'm 80 years old looking back at my life, which choice would I regret more — trying and failing, or never trying at all? He concluded that he'd never regret the attempt, but he'd always regret not trying. The framework forces you to shift your time horizon from the present (where fear lives) to the end of your life (where clarity lives). Most short-term fears look like noise from 80 years out.

## Key insight

Fear lives in the present. Regret lives in the future. Most short-term risks that loom large today become invisible from age 80. The question isn't "what's risky?" It's "what will I wish I had done?"

## Practice prompt

Help me apply the Regret Minimization Framework to a decision I'm wrestling with, where fear or uncertainty is making it hard to think clearly.

The decision I'm facing: [DESCRIBE THE DECISION — what you're considering vs. what you'd be giving up or risking]

Walk me through the framework:

1. THE 80-YEAR VIEW: I'm 80 years old, in a rocking chair, looking back. Describe in vivid, first-person detail what I would think and feel if I had chosen each path. Make it emotionally real, not abstract.

2. THE REGRET ASYMMETRY: What specific things would I regret about each choice? Is the regret from action (trying and failing) or inaction (never trying) more likely to linger?

3. THE FEAR AUDIT: List the fears that are currently influencing this decision. Now — how significant does each one seem from age 80? Which fears were paper tigers?

4. THE ENERGY COST: Even if I choose the safe path, what ongoing energy will I spend on "what if?" How does that compound over decades?

5. THE VERDICT: Based purely on the 80-year view, what does the right choice look like? What would my 80-year-old self say to me right now?

Remove all short-term thinking. Only 80-year-old clarity matters.

## A small challenge

Think of one thing you've been putting off "until the timing is better." Ask your 80-year-old self: will the timing matter? Or will you just wish you had started?
