I want to design habits that don't just add up — they compound. Like compound interest, but for personal effectiveness. Each habit should amplify the others.

My current situation:
- My biggest goal for the next 12 months: [YOUR GOAL]
- My current daily routine (roughly): [YOUR ROUTINE]
- Habits I've tried and failed at before: [PAST FAILURES]
- Time available for new habits: [REALISTIC ESTIMATE]

Design a compound habit stack:
1. THE FOUNDATION HABIT: The one tiny habit (under 2 minutes) that makes everything else easier
2. THE AMPLIFIER: A second habit that gets 2x better because of the first one
3. THE MULTIPLIER: A third habit that becomes almost automatic because of the first two
4. THE TRIGGER CHAIN: How each habit naturally triggers the next
5. THE FAILURE PROTOCOL: What happens when I miss a day? (most systems break here)
6. THE 30/60/90 PLAN: What this looks like at 30 days, 60 days, and 90 days
7. THE IDENTITY SHIFT: The person I'm becoming by doing these habits (not just the outcomes)
8. THE COMPOUND MATH: What does this stack produce if maintained for 1 year? 3 years?

Make each habit so small it feels almost embarrassingly easy to start. That's the point.
