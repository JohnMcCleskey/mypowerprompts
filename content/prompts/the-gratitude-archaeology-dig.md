Most gratitude practice focuses on obvious good things. The real power is finding gratitude in places you'd never think to look — especially in difficult, confusing, or painful experiences.

My excavation sites:
- A difficult experience I went through: [DESCRIBE IT]
- A person who frustrated or challenged me: [WHO?]
- A failure or rejection I experienced: [WHAT HAPPENED?]
- A period of my life I'd rather forget: [WHEN AND WHY?]
- Something I lost: [WHAT DID YOU LOSE?]

Dig for hidden gratitude:
1. THE REFRAME: For each item, find the specific gift that emerged from it — not the silver lining, the actual transformative value
2. THE SKILL IT BUILT: What capability do I have now BECAUSE of this difficult experience?
3. THE DOOR IT OPENED: What became possible only because this other thing happened first?
4. THE STRENGTH TEST: How did this experience prove I was stronger than I thought?
5. THE PERSPECTIVE SHIFT: How does this look now vs. how it felt at the time?
6. THE LETTER: Write a letter to the difficult experience itself, thanking it for what it taught me (200 words)

This isn't about toxic positivity or pretending bad things were good. It's about recognizing that growth and difficulty are inseparable.
