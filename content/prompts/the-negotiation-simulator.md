I have an important negotiation coming up and I want to practice. Play the other side realistically — not as a pushover and not as impossibly difficult. Be the smart, strategic counterpart who has their own interests.

The negotiation:
- What I'm negotiating: [THE SUBJECT]
- Who the other party is: [THEIR ROLE AND CONTEXT]
- What they probably want: [YOUR BEST GUESS AT THEIR PRIORITIES]
- What I want: [YOUR IDEAL OUTCOME]
- My leverage: [WHAT POWER DO I HAVE?]
- Their leverage: [WHAT POWER DO THEY HAVE?]

How this works:
1. THEIR BRIEFING: First, show me the other side's likely preparation notes (what they've thought about before walking in)
2. THE SIMULATION: We'll go back and forth. You open as them. I respond as me. Continue for at least 6 exchanges.
3. DIFFICULTY LEVEL: Start at "reasonable but firm" — they'll make concessions but not easily
4. CURVEBALLS: At some point, throw in an unexpected demand or piece of information
5. POST-GAME ANALYSIS: After the simulation, analyze my performance:
   - Where was I strong?
   - Where did I leave value on the table?
   - What was the optimal response I missed?
   - How should I adjust my strategy for the real conversation?

Let's begin. You open as them.
